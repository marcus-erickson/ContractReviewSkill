# Contract review prompts

Run each of the five prompts below against the contract, one at a time. Every prompt gets the shared instructions first. The `title` of each result in results.json must be the heading shown here, because that heading is what appears above the result on the page.

To change what the skill extracts, edit the prompts and headings in this file. The page supports 1 to 6 results.

## Shared instructions (apply to all five)

> You are reviewing a construction contract for a reader who needs the key points quickly. Use only what the contract says. Do not rely on general industry norms to fill gaps. Write 100 to 150 words of plain prose or a very short list, with no preamble and no closing summary. Cite the section or article number for every point, for example "(Art. 9.3)". If a topic is not addressed, say "Not addressed in this contract" instead of guessing. If a provision is unusual, one-sided, or likely to create exposure for the party who did not draft it, say so in one clause and explain why. Quote exact wording only when the precise phrasing matters, and keep any quote under 15 words.

## 1. Payment Terms

> Identify how and when the contractor gets paid. Cover the pay application schedule and due dates, the number of days to pay after approval, whether payment is conditioned on the owner paying the GC (pay-when-paid or pay-if-paid), the retainage percentage and when it is released, any retainage reduction at substantial completion, and the conditions for final payment. Note any lien waiver requirements tied to payment. Flag anything that could delay or withhold payment, such as broad set-off rights or payment conditioned on third-party actions.

## 2. Changes and Claims

> Explain how changes to scope, price, or time are handled. Cover who can authorize a change, the required form and approval path, deadlines for submitting change requests or notice of a claim, what happens if notice is late (waiver or forfeiture), how pricing is determined (lump sum, unit price, time and materials, markups allowed), and whether the contractor must keep working while a change is disputed. Flag any constructive change language, short notice windows, or provisions that limit recovery for owner-directed extra work.

## 3. Schedule and Delay

> Summarize the schedule obligations and delay remedies. Cover the commencement date, substantial and final completion deadlines, milestone dates, liquidated damages (amount, trigger, and any cap), the process and grounds for time extensions, and whether a no-damages-for-delay clause applies. Note any schedule submission or update requirements and any acceleration rights. Flag who bears the risk for weather, owner delay, and concurrent delay, and any exceptions to the no-damages-for-delay clause.

## 4. Risk Transfer

> Describe how risk is allocated. Cover the indemnification clause (scope, whether it covers the indemnitee's own negligence, and any anti-indemnity limits referenced), insurance requirements by coverage type and limits, additional insured and waiver of subrogation requirements, performance and payment bond requirements, and flow-down provisions that bind subcontractors to prime contract terms. Flag broad-form indemnity, indemnity for the owner's sole negligence, or insurance requirements that appear unusually high or hard to obtain.

## 5. Termination and Disputes

> Summarize how the contract can end and how disagreements are resolved. Cover termination for cause (grounds, notice, and cure period), termination for convenience (notice and what the contractor recovers), the right to suspend work, and what happens to payment on termination. Then cover the dispute process, including notice and escalation steps, mediation or arbitration requirements, litigation venue, governing law, and attorney fee provisions. Flag any one-sided termination rights or requirements that must be completed before a claim can be filed.
